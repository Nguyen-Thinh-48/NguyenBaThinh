## The problem

A premium kitchen-cabinet brand was losing enquiries to response time. Customers ask on
Messenger and in the comments under a Page post, often outside working hours, and a cold
lead by morning is usually a lost one.

A generic chatbot makes this worse rather than better. If it invents a product finish, a
lead time or a price, the brand pays for that mistake. So the requirement was narrower
than "add an LLM": answer from the company's own documents, or do not answer.

  ### Grounded, or silent

  Every incoming message is embedded and used to retrieve the closest chunks from a
  **PostgreSQL + PGVector** store before the model sees it. The agent's instructions
  scope it hard: answer from the retrieved context, stay in the brand's voice, and when
  the knowledge base does not cover the question, say so and escalate rather than
  improvise.

  Multi-turn context comes from **Postgres chat memory**, so a customer who asks "and in
  matte?" three messages later is still talking about the same cabinet door.

  Two details that decide whether this survives contact with production: **echo and
  duplicate filtering**, because the Page's own replies come back through the webhook and
  will happily start an infinite conversation with itself, and **a per-user ON/OFF
  toggle**, so a salesperson can take over a thread and the bot goes quiet for that
  customer only.

  ### The knowledge base maintains itself

  The failure mode of every RAG system is a stale index. If updating what the bot knows
  requires an engineer, it stops happening, and the bot starts confidently quoting last
  season's catalogue.

  So the sync is a trigger, not a task. A **Google Drive watcher** picks up new and
  changed files, splits them into roughly **800-token chunks**, embeds each one with
  Gemini and upserts it into the vector store.

  The person who owns the product information drops a file in a folder. That is the
  entire publishing workflow, and it is why the knowledge base is still current.

## Why self-hosted

The whole stack — n8n and PostgreSQL with the PGVector extension — runs in **Docker
Compose on a single VPS**. That was a deliberate choice over the managed equivalents.

- **The customer conversations stay on infrastructure the company controls.** For a brand
  handling enquiries and contact details, that is not a preference.
- **Cost is a fixed server bill** rather than per-execution pricing that scales with
  exactly the thing you are trying to encourage.
- **Scaling and backup are ordinary ops problems** with ordinary answers, instead of tier
  limits.

The trade is real: nobody else is on call for the VPS. For a workload of this size that
has been the right side of the trade.

## What I built

The n8n workflows, the RAG pipeline and its Drive sync, the agent instructions and answer
